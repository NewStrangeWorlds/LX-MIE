Theory and numerical method¶

This page summarises the equations that are evaluated by LX-MIE and the numerical methods that are used to do so. A detailed description is given in Kitzmann & Heng (2018) [KH18]. Comprehensive introductions to Mie theory can be found in the textbooks by Bohren & Huffman [BH83] and van de Hulst [vdH57].

Mie theory¶

Mie theory [Mie08] describes the scattering and absorption of a plane electromagnetic wave by a homogeneous sphere. The solution only depends on two quantities: the complex refractive index \(m\) of the sphere relative to the surrounding medium and the size parameter

\[x = \frac{2 \pi r}{\lambda} \ ,\]

where \(r\) is the radius of the sphere and \(\lambda\) the wavelength in the surrounding medium.

All optical properties are expressed as infinite series in terms of the Mie coefficients \(a_n\) and \(b_n\). The extinction and scattering efficiencies are given by

\[Q_\mathrm{ext} = \frac{2}{x^2} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left( 2n + 1 \right) \mathrm{Re} \left( a_n + b_n \right) \ ,\]
\[Q_\mathrm{sca} = \frac{2}{x^2} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left( 2n + 1 \right) \left( |a_n|^2 + |b_n|^2 \right) \ .\]

The absorption efficiency follows from \(Q_\mathrm{abs} = Q_\mathrm{ext} - Q_\mathrm{sca}\).

The asymmetry parameter is calculated from

\[g = \frac{4}{x^2 Q_\mathrm{sca}} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \left[ \frac{n \left( n + 2 \right)}{n + 1} \mathrm{Re} \left( a_n a_{n+1}^* + b_n b_{n+1}^* \right) + \frac{2n + 1}{n \left( n + 1 \right)} \mathrm{Re} \left( a_n b_n^* \right) \right] \ ,\]

where the asterisk denotes the complex conjugate.

Scattering amplitudes and phase function¶

The angular distribution of the scattered light is described by the two complex scattering amplitudes

\[S_1(\mu) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2n + 1}{n \left( n + 1 \right)} \left[ a_n \pi_n(\mu) + b_n \tau_n(\mu) \right] \ ,\]
\[S_2(\mu) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{2n + 1}{n \left( n + 1 \right)} \left[ a_n \tau_n(\mu) + b_n \pi_n(\mu) \right] \ ,\]

with the cosine of the scattering angle \(\mu = \cos\theta\) and the angular functions \(\pi_n\) and \(\tau_n\), which are related to the Legendre polynomials and their derivatives. The phase function for unpolarised incident light is

\[p(\mu) = \frac{2}{x^2 Q_\mathrm{sca}} \left( |S_1(\mu)|^2 + |S_2(\mu)|^2 \right) \ .\]

It is normalised such that its average over all directions is unity.

Numerical method¶

The direct evaluation of the Mie coefficients from their definition in terms of spherical Bessel functions is numerically unstable, in particular for large size parameters and strongly absorbing particles. LX-MIE therefore expresses the Mie coefficients by ratios of Riccati-Bessel functions, which can be calculated by stable recurrence relations.

Number of terms¶

The Mie series are truncated after

\[N = x + 4.3 \, x^{1/3} + 2\]

terms (rounded down to an integer). The computing time and the memory required for a calculation therefore increase linearly with the size parameter (see Performance and parallelisation).

Mie coefficients¶

The Mie coefficients are calculated from

\[a_n = C_n(x) \, \frac{A_n(mx) / m - A_n(x)}{A_n(mx) / m - B_n(x)} \ , \qquad b_n = C_n(x) \, \frac{m \, A_n(mx) - A_n(x)}{m \, A_n(mx) - B_n(x)} \ ,\]

where \(A_n\) and \(B_n\) are the logarithmic derivatives of the Riccati-Bessel functions \(\psi_n\) and \(\zeta_n\), respectively, and \(C_n\) is their ratio:

\[A_n(z) = \frac{\psi_n'(z)}{\psi_n(z)} \ , \qquad B_n(x) = \frac{\zeta_n'(x)}{\zeta_n(x)} \ , \qquad C_n(x) = \frac{\psi_n(x)}{\zeta_n(x)} \ .\]

Downward recursion for \(A_n\). The upward recursion of \(A_n\) is unstable. LX-MIE therefore always uses the downward recursion

\[A_{n-1}(z) = \frac{n}{z} - \frac{1}{\dfrac{n}{z} + A_n(z)} \ ,\]

starting at \(n = N\). It is performed for both the complex argument \(z = mx\) and the real argument \(z = x\).

Starting value from continued fractions. The starting value \(A_N(z)\) is calculated with the method of continued fractions by Lentz [Len76]. It is based on the representation of the ratio of two Bessel functions of consecutive order as a continued fraction,

\[A_N(z) = - \frac{N}{z} + \frac{J_{N-1/2}(z)}{J_{N+1/2}(z)} = - \frac{N}{z} + \left[ a_1; a_2, a_3, \ldots \right] \ , \qquad a_k = \left( -1 \right)^{k+1} \frac{2 \left( N + k - 1/2 \right)}{z} \ ,\]

which Lentz’s algorithm evaluates as a product of partial numerators and denominators. The iteration is stopped when the relative difference between the current partial numerator and denominator is smaller than \(10^{-10}\). In contrast to the commonly used approach of starting the downward recursion at an order much larger than \(N\) with an arbitrary value, this method does not require any additional terms and yields the starting value with a known accuracy.

Upward recursion for \(B_n\) and \(C_n\). These two quantities are stable in upward direction and are calculated from

\[B_n(x) = - \frac{n}{x} + \frac{1}{\dfrac{n}{x} - B_{n-1}(x)} \ , \qquad C_n(x) = C_{n-1}(x) \, \frac{B_n(x) + n/x}{A_n(x) + n/x} \ ,\]

with analytical starting values for \(B_0\) and \(C_1\) in terms of trigonometric functions.

Angular functions¶

The angular functions \(\pi_n\) and \(\tau_n\) are calculated by the upward recurrence relations of Wiscombe [Wis79]. With \(\pi_0 = 0\) and \(\pi_1 = 1\),

\[s = \mu \, \pi_n \ , \qquad t = s - \pi_{n-1} \ ,\]
\[\pi_{n+1} = s + \frac{n + 1}{n} \, t \ , \qquad \tau_n = n \, t - \pi_{n-1} \ .\]

Legendre series¶

The coefficients of the Legendre series of the phase function are not obtained by a numerical integration over the scattering angle, but directly from the Mie coefficients, following the procedure of Dave [Dav70]. The accuracy of the coefficients therefore does not depend on the resolution of an angular quadrature.

Sign convention of the refractive index¶

The sign of the imaginary part of the refractive index depends on the chosen time dependence of the electromagnetic wave. LX-MIE internally uses

\[m = n - \mathrm{i} k \ , \qquad k \geq 0 \ .\]

Every refractive index passed to LX-MIE is converted to this convention by replacing the imaginary part with \(-|k|\). Consequently, either sign can be used for the imaginary part. As a result of this convention, the scattering amplitudes are the complex conjugates of those obtained with \(m = n + \mathrm{i} k\), the convention used by Bohren & Huffman [BH83].
